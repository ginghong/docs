Each topic contains two opposing stance tokens. Participants can buy either token, lock it to receive voting power, and use that voting power to participate in the argument layer. The token also acts as a visible affiliation with that side—a foundation for community identity, roles, and recognition.

## Current MVP flow

<Steps>
  <Step title="Discover a topic">
    Browse active debates and compare the market activity, token prices, and leading arguments on both sides.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Choose a side">
    Buy the stance token that best represents your position. Each side has its own bonding curve, and ownership makes your affiliation with that side visible.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Lock tokens">
    Locking stance tokens converts economic commitment into voting power while the tokens remain locked.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Publish an argument">
    A participant with at least **10 voting power** can publish an argument for their side.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Back strong arguments">
    Voting power can be allocated to arguments on the participant's side, helping the most convincing contributions rise.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Share in activity">
    A portion of trading fees is distributed to eligible lockers, connecting sustained participation with market activity.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Market structure

A DualClash topic combines three connected layers:

| Layer | Purpose |
| --- | --- |
| Stance market | Makes relative attention and economic conviction visible through two tradable tokens |
| Argument layer | Directs capital-weighted attention toward the contributions a side considers most persuasive |
| Identity and community layer | Makes affiliation visible and gives holders a foundation for roles, reputation, recognition, and coordination |

The two sides belong to the same topic but maintain separate stance tokens and bonding curves. This allows both sides to grow while preserving a clear comparison between them. The shared token gives each side a recognizable community boundary without requiring the protocol to declare a final winner.

<Note>
  The bonding-curve parameters, fee allocation, lock duration, and eventual liquidity migration remain active design areas. This documentation describes the current MVP direction, not immutable protocol economics.
</Note>

## Example

For the topic **“Is Ethereum L2 dead?”**, the market could launch:

- **L2 IS DEAD** — the stance that rollups are losing relevance
- **L2 LIVES** — the stance that L2s remain essential to Ethereum scaling

Participants buy the token representing their view, lock tokens to gain voting power, and compete to surface the strongest evidence and arguments for their side.

## What the market communicates

DualClash does not produce a final ruling. At any moment, it helps observers answer three different questions:

- Which side has attracted more economic conviction?
- Which arguments are receiving the strongest committed support?
- Is the topic gaining or losing overall attention?

Together, these signals turn a flat debate thread into a living two-sided market.
