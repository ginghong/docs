DualClash is a **two-sided token launchpad for public topics**. Every topic creates two opposing stance tokens, giving people a simple way to choose a side, express conviction, and compete through both capital and arguments.

<Note>
  DualClash is not a prediction market. A topic does not need an objective resolution, and token prices are not claims about truth or probability. They reflect the attention and conviction gathered by each side.
</Note>

## The core idea

Public discussion already behaves like an attention market: a topic appears, communities take sides, arguments spread, and visible support attracts more attention. DualClash gives that behavior a transparent on-chain structure.

## More than a market position

A stance token is also a **social primitive**. It gives people who share a position a visible on-chain identity: holding it signals “this is my side,” while locking it signals deeper commitment.

Over time, that shared affiliation can support side-specific roles, badges, access, and reputation. The token does not only make conviction tradable—it can help holders find one another, organize around a shared position, and earn recognition inside the community they are helping build.

## Why DualClash exists

Most meme tokens begin with attention but struggle to sustain meaning once the next trend arrives. Social platforms generate endless debate, but likes and comments measure engagement more than commitment. Prediction markets work well when an event has a verifiable outcome, but many important public disagreements never resolve cleanly.

DualClash explores a different primitive: **a market for public conviction**. It connects a two-sided market with an argument layer so that trading, participation, and content can reinforce one another.
