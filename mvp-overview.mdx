The DualClash MVP is a focused product experiment: can a public disagreement become a two-sided market where people express conviction, form communities, and strengthen their position through arguments?

It is designed to make the concept understandable in one session. A user should be able to discover a topic, compare both sides, buy a stance token, lock it for participation rights, and support the arguments they find most persuasive.

## Product promise

> Choose a side, make your conviction visible, and help your side build the strongest case.

## Who the MVP serves

| User | What they want | What DualClash gives them |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Participant | Take a visible position on a live topic | A stance token and side identity |
| Contributor | Persuade others and earn recognition | Argument publishing and voting |
| Supporter | Help good ideas rise without writing a post | Voting power to back arguments |
| Observer | Understand where attention and conviction are moving | A comparison of both markets and their leading arguments |

## The core loop

<Steps>
  <Step title="A topic attracts attention">
    Two clearly opposed stance tokens give the discussion a simple structure.
  </Step>
  <Step title="People choose and buy a side">
    Trading makes relative economic conviction visible and brings holders into recognizable communities.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Committed holders lock tokens">
    Locking converts holdings into voting power and unlocks deeper participation.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Arguments compete for support">
    Contributors publish arguments while holders direct voting power toward the strongest reasoning.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Content creates more attention">
    Strong arguments and visible market movement give people something worth sharing, bringing new participants into the topic.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## What the MVP should validate

The first version does not need to prove the complete protocol economy. It needs to test whether:

- new users understand the two-token structure without mistaking it for a prediction market;
- buying a token feels like expressing a stance, not merely making a wager;
- locking creates a meaningful transition from holder to participant;
- arguments give the tokens more context and conviction than a typical meme token;
- identity and recognition make holders feel part of a side;
- the combined market-and-content loop is interesting enough to revisit and share.

## Current product boundaries

DualClash does not determine which side is objectively correct. Topics do not require a final resolution, and token prices should not be read as probabilities. The MVP visualizes attention, economic conviction, participation, and the arguments supported by each community.
