> **Thesis:** Public debate already functions as an attention market. DualClash gives it an on-chain market structure: two opposing stance tokens make conviction visible, while arguments give that conviction meaning.

## Attention alone is fragile

Meme tokens can turn culture and attention into liquid markets, but attention moves quickly. When a token has no meaning beyond the meme itself, holders often have little reason to stay once the next narrative appears.

Public disagreements behave differently. People do not only notice a topic—they identify with a side, defend it, and persuade others. That creates a deeper source of participation: **conviction**.

## Disagreement has a natural market structure

Most public topics already form two competing camps:

- Is Ethereum L2 dead or still essential?
- Which stablecoin is safer: USDC or USDT?
- Should AI development be accelerated or slowed down?

Each side produces arguments, communities, and attention. DualClash represents those sides with separate stance tokens so their relative support becomes visible in real time.

## From engagement to commitment

Likes, reposts, and polls are cheap signals. Buying and locking a stance token adds cost and persistence to the signal.

This does not make capital equal to truth. It makes commitment legible. The market shows where attention and conviction are gathering, while the argument layer shows **why**.

<Info>
  Token price is a signal of market attention and commitment—not an objective measure of truth, moral value, or probability.
</Info>

## The conviction loop

<Steps>
  <Step title="A debatable topic attracts attention">
    A clear two-sided question gives communities something concrete to support or oppose.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Participants choose a side">
    People buy a stance token to make their position economically visible.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Arguments deepen the market">
    Token holders publish and back arguments, turning a position into shareable content.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Content brings new attention">
    Strong arguments spread beyond the market, attracting new participants and trading activity.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Participation reinforces conviction">
    Voting power and fee sharing give committed participants a reason to remain involved.
  </Step>
</Steps>

The intended result is a self-reinforcing system in which **topics create markets, markets fund attention, and arguments give that attention meaning**.

## A different primitive from prediction markets

Prediction markets ask, “What will happen?” and eventually depend on an oracle or a verifiable outcome.

DualClash asks, “What do people stand for, and how strongly?” Many cultural, political, technological, and community debates remain open-ended. DualClash is designed for those topics: markets can stay active without declaring one side objectively correct.
