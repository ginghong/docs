The MVP should tell a coherent story from curiosity to participation. Every step needs to answer one question for the user before asking them to take the next action.

## First-session journey

<Steps>
  <Step title="Discover a topic">
    The user sees a clear question, two opposing sides, current market activity, and a preview of the strongest arguments.

    **The user should understand:** what people disagree about and why the topic is active.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Compare both sides">
    The topic page presents both stance tokens symmetrically, without implying that price equals truth.

    **The user should understand:** what each token represents and how support currently differs.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Choose a stance">
    The user selects the side that matches their view and previews the token amount, price impact, fees, and resulting position.

    **The user should understand:** buying expresses economic conviction and creates visible affiliation.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Complete the trade">
    The interface confirms the transaction and immediately reflects the new balance, side identity, and market movement.

    **The user should understand:** they now hold a position and belong to that side's holder community.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Lock for voting power">
    The user chooses how many tokens to lock and sees the voting power they will receive and any applicable unlock conditions.

    **The user should understand:** locking is a deeper commitment that unlocks participation.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Publish or back an argument">
    Eligible users can write an argument. Other holders can allocate voting power to contributions on their side.

    **The user should understand:** their position can influence which reasoning represents the community.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Share the position">
    The user shares the topic, their stance, or a strong argument to bring others into the discussion.

    **The user should understand:** market movement and argument quality evolve together as attention grows.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Returning-user loop

A returning participant should quickly see what changed since the last visit:

- price, market cap, holders, and activity for both sides;
- new or rising arguments within their side;
- voting power already allocated and still available;
- position value, locked balance, and any accumulated rewards;
- replies, recognition, or role changes connected to their contributions.

## Critical moments to explain well

| Moment | Likely confusion | Product response |
| --- | --- | --- |
| First view of two tokens | “Is this a prediction market?” | State that prices reflect conviction and attention, not outcome probability |
| Buying a side | “What exactly am I receiving?” | Show token balance, side identity, price impact, and fees before confirmation |
| Locking | “Am I staking, voting, or both?” | Separate locked balance, voting power, eligibility, rewards, and unlock rules |
| Voting on arguments | “Does my vote move the token price?” | Explain that votes rank arguments; trades determine the market state |
| Comparing sides | “Does the higher market cap mean it is true?” | Use neutral language such as leading in support or economic conviction |
